Plate book

The figure work for the benchmark battery, after review: four plates adopted into benchmark/ as normal plotters, three parked. Everything is rendered from the real repository data — geometry out of structures.py, numbers out of night_run_2 — so no figure can drift from what it claims to draw.

13 structures 5 code+rule columns 1 345 measured solves 1 … 961 retained orders λ = 1, lengths in µm

Decided: S2 for every structure with S3 as the opener, C2 and C4 for the convergence, colour = rule. Click any figure to inspect it full size.

Part A · shipped

Drawing the structures

One style for all thirteen, plus the atlas as an opener. The decisive change from the first round: air is absent rather than grey. The gaps between ridges and pillars are open space, a perforated film is cut through to its substrate, and a free-standing case gets a dashed phantom half space instead of a solid block — so what you see filled is exactly what is material.

S2adopted

Every structure as a solid isometric unit cell

Ridges, pillars and cylinders are real prisms with shaded faces and depth-sorted overlap, not a textured block. A hole array is drawn as a pit you look into: far walls in shadow, substrate at the bottom. E is drawn along the axis the polarization actually selects, so TE-along-the-grooves versus TM-across-them reads as geometry rather than as a letter you have to decode.

Part B · shipped

Drawing the convergence

Both adopted plates drop the red/green encoding: colour is now the factorization rule and dash plus marker the codebase, on the argument that the story this battery tells is direct-versus-faithful rather than fork-versus-ikarus. Both Laurent columns therefore collapse onto one grey — which is itself the claim that the direct rule behaves identically in both codes. The palette is Okabe-Ito, so the figures survive colour-blind readers and greyscale printing.

C2adopted

The cost staircase: the best error a wall-time budget can buy

Faint markers are the raw measurements; the thick step is the running best — the honest answer to “with 100 ms, how close can I get?”. Monotone by construction, so the oscillation that makes a plain error-vs-time scatter unreadable becomes a flat tread instead of a scribble. A flat tread means spending more buys nothing.

C4adopted · two axes

Signed deviation on a symlog axis

One linear axis cannot hold both the low-order transient and the 1e-4 endgame, which is why the raw figure needs a _tight twin. A symlog axis holds both: outside ±1e-4 it is logarithmic and you read the transient and the sign of the approach; inside the band it goes linear and the endgame is legible in the same panel. Two variants — against retained orders, with Moose overlaid in black, and against wall time, where the external reference cannot appear because it carries no timings. The summary bar strip from the first draft is gone.

C1parked

The digit map

One row per code+rule, one colour step per correct digit of R, ▼ at the first sustained |ΔR| ≤ 1e-4. Fastest glance of anything here — group C is visibly a wall of blue, nobody converges — but it throws away the sign and the shape of the decay, which is what C2 and C4 keep. Left in viz_proposals/.
